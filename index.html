Theory: Multiple Integrals

Multiple integrals extend single-variable integration to compute accumulation over regions in 2D and 3D.

1Double Integration

I = ∬R f(x,y) dA

A double integral adds up values of a function over a plane region R, evaluated as an iterated integral.

∬R f(x,y) dA = ∫ab ∫g₁(x)g₂(x) f(x,y) dy dx
1Sketch/identify the region R from limits.
2Decide the integration order (dy dx or dx dy).
3Evaluate inner integral, then outer integral.
Example∬0≤x≤1, 0≤y≤2 (x+y) dA = ∫01 ∫02 (x+y) dy dx

2Conversion to Polar Form

x = r cosθ,  y = r sinθ,  dA = r dr dθ
∬R f(x,y) dA = ∫∫ f(r cosθ, r sinθ) · r dr dθ
→Convert region: describe R using r and θ bounds.
→Convert function: substitute x = r cosθ, y = r sinθ.
→Include Jacobian r — essential for correct result.
Disk Example∬x²+y²≤a² 1 dA = ∫02π ∫0a r dr dθ = πa²

3Application to Area

Area(R) = ∬R 1 dA = ∫αβ ∫r₁(θ)r₂(θ) r dr dθ
Quarter CircleArea = ∫0π/2 ∫0a r dr dθ = πa²/4

4Triple Integration

J = ∭V f(x,y,z) dV = ∫∫∫ f(x,y,z) dz dy dx
1Describe solid V using inequalities or projections.
2Choose integration order with simplest bounds.
3Integrate: inner → middle → outer.

5Dirichlet's Theorem

Useful for integrals over simplex regions (x,y,z ≥ 0, x+y+z ≤ a). Converts integrals into products of Gamma functions.

∭ xp-1 yq-1 zr-1 dV = ap+q+r · Γ(p)Γ(q)Γ(r) / Γ(p+q+r+1)

Used as a standard result to quickly evaluate such integrals in engineering mathematics.

6Application to Volume

Box0≤x≤a, 0≤y≤b, 0≤z≤c → Volume = abc
